# Shared Eden routes: one setup library for every lane

2026-11-16. `tools/eden-runner/routes.py` (Training and menus) and `routes_online.py` (pairs) hold the setup
blocks every Eden row reuses. Before them, each lane hand-built its boot route and fixtures. On 2026-11-15 the
TrainingRooms rc.15 lanes and the Online Deluxe lane lost more than eight boots to setup mistakes this playbook
already covered ([HARNESS-LESSONS](HARNESS-LESSONS.md)).

## What is proven, and when to use it

- **Proven hosts** (2026-11-16, "Proof" below): the XPS and the Surface (Linux) and the Ally (Windows). The Yoga
  hasn't booted these blocks; its proof boot was deferred because the feature lanes have slot priority. Proven
  blocks:
  - `configure_boot`, `load_game`, `wait_lines`, `to_main`;
  - `main_to_stage`, `pick_stage` (by name);
  - `pick_fighters` for P1 only;
  - `enter_training`, `chord`, `fixture`, `back_to_main`.
- **Not proven:** the CPU's coin on the CSS, damage cleared from above 0, and the online blocks.
- **A lane's first proof boot** may reuse a route that already passed for it, verbatim; for TrainingRooms that is
  rc15-integration remaining-2's boot → Training (no CSS/SSS walk). Use the blocks for the rows after it, for
  example a stage by name or a fixture per row.
- **A one-off Eden glitch is not a route bug.** Down, rerun once, and note it. Change a block only when the failure
  repeats.

## How to call a block

A block is a runner step hook. A row names it with `routes:` and no path:

```json
{"run": "routes:pick_stage", "args": {"stage": "Battlefield"}, "check": true}
```

- Every block checks the screen it starts on, so it never trusts a gate from an earlier screen.
- Every block waits on game state with a cap: Eden `status`, a plugin line, a tile-cursor line or an image gate.
  It sleeps a fixed time only where no state exists.
- Every block ends on a still in the row folder and returns a small receipt. Stills are reviewed after the run; a
  block never stops for a live review (the game would idle into its attract demo).
- Block receipts are plain JSON values; the runner stringifies anything else rather than failing the row.
- Every block fails closed: a missing state raises with what was seen, and nothing guesses.

### Own steps

Any setup item or step may include a one-line `reason`. A reason does not make a library step private.
Dry-run reports private entries with their reasons and lists `missing_reasons` by location; missing reasons
do not change readiness. Private steps remain allowed and require full review.

The data the blocks use is in `tools/eden-runner/library/routes/`:

- `index.json`: the macros, each with its sha256 and source. `routes.play()` refuses a file whose hash differs.
- `tables.json`: fighter kinds by CSS tile and stage ids by stage-select tile. It is generated by `build_tables.py`
  from TrainingRooms' own `roster_grid.rs` and `lobby_stages.rs`, never from memory.

### Closed-loop steps (`closed_loop.py`)

Look for the current screen and start there; wait for the step's fresh ready observation before acting.
Before every input, the step requires a `macro.status` of `idle` or `finished` without an error.
`countdown`, `recording`, `playing`, and `branch_playing` wait; any other or unreadable status is treated as busy.
One 15-second budget per pending input spans re-reads and rejected dispatches, and a slow status reply requires
a fresh read. Each `macro.status` poll is limited to the remaining budget; its control-client timeout cannot
extend that budget, and an unanswered poll counts as busy. Stall diagnostic `status` reads are capped at one
second and skipped on idle and stop paths. Only a refusal of the `macro.play` call itself (`input_busy`) is
retried once after idle; a failure after the macro was accepted stops as delivery unknown. Accumulated waits over
one second and dispatch rejections
are stall events, carrying `kind`, `step`, `seconds`, `why`, `last_bridge_line`, and `utc`; idle waits also carry
their count in `waits`.
The wireless route reserves 4.5 seconds for revalidation, leaving 10.5 seconds of the 15-second budget for waiting;
if that reserve is unavailable it stops without reading or sending.
The Stage Select A gate requires a settled `tile.cursor` with numeric `at == hovered` and both `p1.token`
and `local.token` held. Every wireless press also requires `input.ready=yes` whenever the bridge publishes that
key; never press on the screen name alone.
On the room list, A requires `focus.id=ldn.create_room` and `focus.index=0`: a room row or unknown focus stops;
the route never moves that cursor.
After a send, wait for the transition. A continuously observed source with the same target counts as a lost input:
finish it and resend, up to three sends total (some steps allow fewer). A continuously changed target stops without
confirming or repeating the input. A dispatch error stops with delivery unknown, without confirming or resending.
A bridge continuity, boot or fence error is never retried: the route stops on the current step with the receipt and leaves the input unconfirmed.
If observations stay unreadable or in flight until the cap, stop on the named step without confirming or pressing again.
Every wireless bridge read gets at least 2 seconds. An unanswered read preserves the last answered read and the
source timer, but cannot authorise input; at the deadline one grace read may witness completion or a qualified retry.
Training settle reads also have a 2-second floor and may finish up to 2 seconds after their settle cap.
The Create form is refused by default until the bridge publishes active `ldn.create`;
`unpublished_create='allow'` is an explicit, recorded exception.
A step's sends are counted for the whole route run, so a bounce cannot restart its budget.
Training phases re-locate after each phase; a failed phase retries only after two consecutive fresh reads of a
recognised native-owned screen, with at most three starts per phase per call. Continuity errors and unreadable
state stop with `routes-reach-training-stop.json`. The overall cap is checked between phases; a running phase ends
on its own caps. This phase recovery is host-tested only.
A Training fighter select reached without a recorded stage is not trusted: the route returns to Main and picks the stage again.
`ready` is sent once; a second Plus stays out until a flown run has measured that load.
Wireless stall events record `step`, `send`, `seconds_on_source`, `target`, `screen`, `frame`, `wall`, `rev`,
`tile`, `last_bridge_line`, `eden_frame_ms` and available `eden_fps`, and `utc`. Training retry/recovery events
record `kind`, `step`, `start`, `error`, `screen`, `seconds`, `frame`, `wall`, `rev`, `last_bridge_line` and `utc`.
Receipts say `PASS`, `PASS with 1 stall`, or `PASS with N stalls` (and corresponding `STOPPED` forms on failure);
the row folder also receives `stall-events.jsonl`. The flight owner copies that file into the root-cause folder.
Inner Training input resends are stall events in every enclosing receipt and in `stall-events.jsonl`.
After a phase fails, the route re-decides from live state and never repeats it when its postcondition already holds.
This closed-loop route is host-tested only, not flown.

**Why (2026-11-23/24):** the three stalled flights pressed A 5 to 12 frames after the first `stage_select` block,
before the picker took input, and the wait's last read had a 1 ms cap; see
`smash-training-lobby/artifacts/phase23/route-hardening/stage-stall/ROOT-CAUSE.md`.
The `lobby-a` press stays 9 frames (commit 2537dd5). Its stated reason, that a 4-frame tap did not confirm, was a
misreading of the early press: both lengths are lost when sent before the picker takes input, and a 4-frame tap sent
late did reach the fighter select (proof-lobby-17060a5e-3). 9 frames is kept only because it is the length flown in
every later successful transition; it is not evidence against the 4-frame tap.

`to_main` defaults to a 54-second navigation cap. If it observes native `title.opening_playback` with no dialog
within that initial wait, it allows one passive opening-readiness period, capped at 180 seconds from route entry.
The unchanged navigation cap starts when the next qualified prompt or Main appears. The opening grants no input.
The route then sends Plus immediately after freshly fencing the native Title prompt, once. A qualified How to Play
skip remains a separate single-shot recovery within the navigation deadline.

`pick_stage` and `pick_fighters` step the tile cursor ([TILE-CURSOR](TILE-CURSOR.md)) with the `tile-*` macros,
never a plain D-pad. Each step must log `step … to=N`, then `settled … at=N`. Row widths come from the log, not
from a constant.

### The CPU's coin: not proven

With P1 placed, `pick_cpu` steps the empty hand to the CPU coin and checks `hand.token=1` before A. It verifies that
the CPU coin is held, walks it through the pinned CSS topology and verifies the target identity after placement.
If the coins overlap and the hand points to P1's coin, it first parks P1's coin on a qualified tile, selects the
CPU, then returns P1's coin and verifies the original medal. Unexpected state or an older Tile Cursor without the
coin lines stops the route. The former stick-based `coin_route` is not supported.

2026-11-22: this token-step route is host-tested on modelled blocks and has not been flown.

## Game state (TrainingRooms diag build)

- **Bodies:** `read_state` writes `diag-pos.req` beside `settings.json`. TrainingRooms answers with
  `[TrainingRooms] state pos entry=N kind= x= y= lr= dmg= status= situation=`, where entry 0 is P1 and entry 1 is
  the CPU. Only lines after the request count.
- **Stage:** the stage id comes from the camera lines `[TrainingRooms] cpu_event camera_setup|camera_mode … stage=N`.
- **Settled:** status 0 and situation 0, with x/y unchanged across two fresh reads, as in remaining-2-d1's approved
  predicate. That predicate covered P1 only; this one covers both bodies.
- **Tracing:** with the GDB stub on, tracing is off, so these reads come from the plugin's lines.

**Release builds: the plugin's own text.** `trace_text.py` reads pane names and text from text-setter trace records;
arm the point in a boot step with the stub off, `trace.configure` and the same-build ELF. A `text` gate matches the
newest text for a pane in the instance's `trace_jsonl`, optionally bound to a `point_id`. By default it reads records
written during the row; `"whole_file": true` includes earlier records. Any newer record that concerns the pane but
cannot be read (text, length, sequence, address), and any trace loss line, leaves the pane's text unknown until a newer
readable record. An empty request is read as empty text. Transition `measured` has earlier different text on the same
pane key; `unmeasured` has the same earlier text without a different observation; `sampled` has no earlier observation
on that key and shows only that text was requested. Transition and `previous` come from the gate's scope only, and
`before_scope` gives the newest readable text on the same pane key before it. This reader and gate are host-tested on a
real trace excerpt, not yet proven in a live boot.
At the setter's entry, a text record is a request and proves neither that the text was written nor that the pane is
visible or focused. Bind `point_id` to the game's write-call point when a write must be shown, and use a still or
another state source for visibility, focus and positions.

## Logs that rotate

`routes.LogCursor(path)` keeps the file it started on open:
- **Rotation:** on a rename it drains the old file, then reads the new one from its start.
- **Truncation:** when the file shrinks, it reads from 0.
- **Waiting:** `wait(regex, cap)` returns the first new matching line and keeps later lines for the next wait.

Use it for any plugin log a wait reads. TrainingRooms' `practice-diagnostics.log` rotates at 128 KiB.

## Online pairs (`routes_online`)

These checks are taken from the runs that passed: runtime-calibration-4 (LAN, 2026-11-15) and
eden-ldn-coexistence runtime-12/13 (Local Wireless). They have not been booted again as blocks.

- **`lan_leader`:** after A's `game.load`, A's console shows:
  - one `[auto] broker started … local_only=false`;
  - one `[coord] leading`;
  - one `[auto] ready pid=P`;
  - `member joined pid=P … members=1`.
- **`lan_pair`:** after B's `game.load`, A's last `members=` is 2 with B's ready pid, and B has no `leading` line.
  If B leads its own room, the block fails, and both instances go Down before Quickplay.

- **`ldn_join`** keeps native Join closed until an exact-release trace qualifies

  approval macros remain historical setup evidence.
- **`ldn_pick_fighter`** selects a named fighter from a verified native CSS
  belonging to the local host or guest. The later L+R Start is a distinct action.

## A real Switch (the same rows, the pad and OBS)

Rows for the console use the same `rows.json` format, segments, gates and `routes:` hooks as Eden. Only the
instance differs, and every input is a macro (no manual presses).

```text
/usr/bin/python3 -I tools/eden-runner/recipe_macro.py rows.json            # recipes -> .edenmacro.json + sha256
/usr/bin/python3 -I tools/eden-runner/runner.py dry-run rows.json          # READY before the console
/usr/bin/python3 -I tools/eden-runner/switch_hw.py up <run dir>            # claim, pad, OBS, live Skyline log
/usr/bin/python3 -I tools/eden-runner/runner.py run rows.json --instances <run dir>/instances.json --out <run dir>/out
/usr/bin/python3 -I tools/eden-runner/switch_hw.py down <run dir>          # always
```

- **Input:** `usb:///dev/ttyACM0` (the ESP32-S3 pad, switch-pad 0.1.2) goes to switch-remote's client
  (`SWITCH_REMOTE_DIR`, default `~/ssbu-switch-remote/tools/host`).
  - `macro.play` uploads the Eden macro with `MBEGIN`/`MEV` and the pad plays it at 60 Hz of its own clock, so
    same-frame chords stay same-frame.
  - The client pings every 300 ms; the pad's 1 s watchdog then never fires mid-macro.
  - HOME and Capture are refused inside macros; the `switch_boot` and `switch_close` hooks press them.
- **Recipes:** `recipe_macro.py` compiles them offline. `b+up` is one frame, and every hold is at least 2 frames.
  The runner never records on hardware.
- **Logs:** `switch_hw.py` captures Skyline's TCP log (`192.168.0.224:6969`) one complete line at a time into the
  instance's `console_log`, so log gates and `LogCursor` work as on Eden.
  - The card runs the RELEASE NRO: TrainingRooms prints its install line and `cpu_common_load` there, and no diag
    lines. Menus and Training need image gates.
  - SD files (`events.log`, settings) are FTP reads after the session, not live gates.
- **Images:** `ui.screenshot` is an OBS still of the capture card. The `routes` gates read it against `hw-*`
  references (`library/routes/refs/`), cut from the reviewed switch-hw-ledger-rc15 stills:
  - each one passes on its own screen and fails on the others (`hw-refs.json`);
  - the Eden references don't apply there (window grabs are 1280x674; the card is a true 16:9 frame).
- **Auto-dim (a calibration on the operator's review list):** after about 5 min without input the console dims
  its screen to about 1/3.
  - When the plain compare fails, the `hw-*` gates compare again with the ROI lifted to the reference's brightness
    (gain 1.3–4).
  - Every pass also needs a third of the reference's contrast, so a flat frame can't pass.
  - `test_routes.py` (SwitchDiscriminationTests) pins it: each gate passes only its own screen, at normal and
    dimmed brightness; every other gate's content at its ROI fails, HOME vs main vs TR menu vs HUD included.
  - **The root alternative:** stop the console from dimming. That's a console setting, and the operator's choice.
- **One recording per session** (plan key `"session_recording": {"marks": ["row-start", "row-end", "shot"],
  "chapters": true, "log": "recording.jsonl"}`), instead of a `gif` step per moment, which cost about 7 s each on
  hardware.
  - **Start and stop:** the runner starts the backend's session recording before the first executed row and stops
    it in `run()`'s `finally`. The backend is switch-remote's `recording.session_*`, over OBS.
  - **Marks:** every executed row is marked at its start and end (failed rows included), and so is every `shot`
    step. Each mark is one JSON line in `<out>/recording.jsonl` (`event`, `row`, `label`, `wall`, `rec_ms`,
    `frame`). Receipts carry `rec_ms: [start, end]`. Clips and stills are cut from the one recording afterwards.
  - **If the runner died:** `switch_hw.py down` stops a recording that this run started and never stopped. OBS is
    shared, so nothing else is touched.
  - **Clips:** `/usr/bin/python3 tools/eden-runner/cut_session.py <out> [--only gifs|rows|stills]` cuts each clip from
    the recording into its preset. It needs PyGObject/GStreamer, so run it without `-I`. Use its GIF output for
    image previews and for existing GIF plans. Keep the 1080p recording and a 2–3× zoom crop for evidence. Encode
    documentation and tutorial MP4s from the original recording ([GIFS](GIFS.md)). Live proof: live-rec-1,
    2026-11-16.
  - **Backends without it:** the run goes on without a session recording. Eden has no `recording.session_*`
    methods today.
  - **One file per row** (`"per_row_files": true`, optional `"split_cap_s": 10`): the row-start mark becomes a
    split (`recording.session_split`: OBS `SplitRecordFile`, then the `RecordFileChanged` event). OBS splits at its
    next keyframe, so the row's input waits for the event. The file that began at row N's split is row N's clip;
    it closes at the next split or at the stop (a failed or interrupted row too). Then the runner hard-links it
    to `<out>/<row>/clip.mp4`, puts `clip` in the row's receipt, and logs a `clip` event in `recording.jsonl`.
    `cut_session.py` then cuts each row's GIFs and stills from its own clip.
    - **A split with no event within the cap fails loudly:** the row-start mark carries `split.ok: false`, the
      row gets no clip, and the open file goes on to hold that row too (`rows` in its `clip` event). There's
      never a Start/Stop instead mid-session: that race was F04 (obs_500/501).
    - **Precondition:** OBS's record output needs manual splitting on. Simple output mode has no UI for it, and
      it resets when OBS restarts or Settings > Output is applied. `switch_hw.py up` checks it and refuses while
      it's off. The fix is `obsws.py split-enable` (switch-remote tools/host); `up --allow-no-split` runs without
      it. The runner itself never changes OBS settings.
- **The bridge NRO (optional, 2026-11-17):** with the test-only `ssbu-hw-bridge` installed beside TrainingRooms,
  `switch_hw.py up <run> --bridge-host 192.168.0.224[:6970]` gives the instance Eden's keys too: `diag_log` is
  `<run>/bridge.log` (TR's own oracle lines plus ` frame= wall=`, also appended to `console_log`), `settings` is
  `<run>/bridge/settings.json` (fetched after every `settings.json written` line), and the folder beside it is a
  request spool where `diag-pos.req`, `diag-save.req`, `diag-load.req`, `diag-item.req` and `diag-settings-next.json`
  become the bridge's `pos`, `save`, `load`, `item`, `set` requests. So `read_state`, settings fixtures and log gates run
  as on Eden. `switch_hw.py state <run> [--fresh pos,speed,roster,status]` (or `switch_hw.bridge_state(run, fresh)` in
  a row) returns the console's newest B-FRAME, B-POS (both bodies), B-STAGE, B-ROSTER, B-ITEM, B-SPEED and
  B-STATUS-NAMES as JSON, without an OBS still; `--fresh` asks the bridge first and waits for the answers. In a row,
  `routes.native_state(ctx, fresh)`, `routes.wait_native(ctx, test, cap)` and `routes.frame_advancing(ctx)` read the
  same through the instance's `bridge_log`. Design:
  `ssbu-hw-bridge/docs/DESIGN.md`.
- **One driver per console:** `up` refuses while any process holds the tty or the claim file
  (`~/.cache/switch-pad.claim`) exists. `down` sends RESET, stops the log capture and removes the claim.
- **Hardware blocks:**
  - `switch_boot`: B, HOME, the SSBU tile gate, A, the profile A, then Start until the main menu.
  - `switch_to_training` starts from the native Games & More hover or the Training
    submenu item. It confirms the Training stage, verifies local fighter placement
    and checks native Training and the loaded stage kind. The native route
    requires a new gameplay stage event after the CSS launch checkpoint.
    It verifies same-boot event replay and rejects gaps, reconnects and old
    event clocks. If the bridge emits no event on a same-stage return,
    the route stops. A repeatable proof needs a new gameplay stage receipt
    for every loaded Training battle, even when the numeric ID is unchanged.
    Supply `p1` and
    `fighter_topology` when a named pick needs movement from an off-grid hand.
  - `tr_menu`: `preload_clear`, B+Up, the menu gate, B and B, the HUD gate.
  - `preload_clear` (`{"run": "routes:preload_clear"}`, or `routes.preload_clear(ctx)` from a hook, on the
    Training HUD before any menu input):
    - **Why:** TR's preloader builds the planned fighters after a Training scene starts, and pad input sent
      meanwhile is lost. In `switch-hw-session-1` T-01 (15:03Z), an R into the TR menu was lost at "Loading 4/7",
      12 s after the HUD.
    - **What it gates on:** the notice in the middle of the screen. "Fighters / Loading n/m" means it's still
      working. Any other "Fighters" state ("Ready 8/8", "All loaded (..)", "Loaded n/m", "Swap .. to load") means
      it's done.
    - **An absent box proves nothing.** The box disappears between steps (90 frames per state), and the HUD comes
      up about 3 s before the first step. The release build logs none of it.
    - **No preload:** with no Loading notice within `appear` (10 s) and no `[NRO hook] Loaded` line meanwhile,
      the gate passes.
    - **Later calls:** a pass is kept in `out/routes-preload.json`, so later calls pass at once until the log
      gains a `[NRO hook] Loaded` line (a new scene or a fighter change).
    - **References and proof:** `hw-notice-fighters`, `hw-notice-loading` (from a session-1 frame). Host-tested
      on synthetic frames, and checked against the session's real frames (Loading 3/7 and 4/7, Ready 8/8, a
      minigame notice, the HUD, the menu). It hasn't run live yet, and it's unproven on Eden.

`routes:tr_open(page=None, cap=20)` opens the Training Mod menu and optionally returns to its page ring
and reaches the named page. `routes:tr_close(cap=20)` backs out through each depth and closes it; it does
not read settings. Both check the bridge owner after their last input, record the observed owner from a
same-epoch `ssbu.ui` sample, check each handled menu input and report skipped work. These
blocks and the bridge-based navigation and drill blocks below are host-tested only, not yet proof-booted.
Each of these eight blocks writes `ok: true` on success. Failure receipts have `ok: false`, the error,
the last values read without a further bridge request, and any partial observations, never `verified`.
Repeated calls in the same row retain earlier receipts with numbered suffixes. When an input has no
observed effect for three seconds, the block retries once; receipt metrics count retries.

`routes:tr_goto(page, path, cap=30)` opens the menu and walks action IDs, or objects containing `action` and/or
the static `row` title, into nested Rows screens. Titles match after NFKC normalization, case folding and
whitespace collapse; action IDs remain exact. It stops before activating the last row. At page depth a
Down must move the published cursor; in a Rows screen it must change the published `(action,row,row_id)` identity
with a handled ACK, because the outer cursor does not move there. A repeated identity fails closed. This does
not read or establish the focused row's stored value.

`routes:tr_set(page, path, kind, value, cap=45)` accepts `list`, `toggle`, `checklist`, or `practice_cpu`.
It closes the menu, reads `settings.json` through the bridge, navigates to the row, changes it as needed,
closes the menu and compares a fresh file read. Changed values require a fresh writer line; already-stored
values skip both the change and writer and must match again on the final read. Source-pinned row kinds
prevent a no-op from silently accepting a wrong kind. `practice_cpu` takes `fighter`, `mode` and optional
`level`; popup mode is witnessed while changing, while a no-op proves only the stored encoding. It does
not prove the live fighter was switched or text visibly rendered. Settings read-back rejects duplicate
keys and values other than arrays of integer 0/1 bits. A toggle also accepts boolean or case-insensitive
`on`/`off`; a list also accepts a stored slot index. The receipt retains the original argument and
`value_stored`. Toggle, list and checklist widths are pinned to the product schema in
`library/routes/tr-setting-widths.json`; the pre-read must have that exact width, which the receipt records.

`routes:wizard_place(game, area=None, cpu=None, p1=None, cap=120, pulse_cap=2.0)` reaches the
Guard the Edge Preview using page bodies and bridged notice headers, independent of the page count. An
`area` request needs an Area page; CPU and P1 targets must equal the shown whole units.
The block sends fixed library stick pulses and leaves Preview unconfirmed. `pulse_cap` is a
harness publication-wait bound, not a product tolerance. Receipts list pages, observed owners, each pulse's
publication status and the number of unconfirmed pulses; scope is published coordinates only, not input
readiness, and any observed `native.pause` page is listed as a limitation. An unpublished pulse may have moved the point by
less than a shown unit; only shown coordinates are verified. Exact position words are available from the
release marker. Notice text is a request, not proof it was visibly rendered.

`routes:drill_stop(game, cap=30)` refuses a visible wizard and a different or stopped practice game. It opens
the Guard the Edge Start row, presses A, requires a same-epoch `game.running=0`, and closes the menu. The
published row title is static Start even when the UI draws Stop; it is not used as a stop witness.

`routes:drill_start(game, expect=None, cap=60)` accepts only Guard the Edge. After input, and again before
the receipt, it requires the menu closed and the requested game running; on the wizard path the wizard
heading must also have disappeared.
With trace enabled it records the `trace.list` sequence fence taken immediately before the start input,
the observed state used for it, the first release after that fence, pre-fence markers and post-fence
terminals preceding the first release. Without trace it can only run when `expect` is omitted.
Expect keys are `cpu_kind`, `stage_form`, `stage_id`, `perfect`, `juggler`, `damage`, `cpu`, `cpu_words`,
`p1`, `p1_words` and `plan`. Positions compare whole-unit shown strings; damage and words compare exactly.
A `plan` check needs the armed symbolic `planner_chose` point and holds the block until the first attempt's
terminal matching the release's ordinal, game and Perfect bits. The release must match the requested game;
CPU kind is not bound across the attempt. `'none'` requires no plan in the attempt and zero planner hits; a hit without
a record is reported as not observed. The sequence fence assumes no recorded point has been removed
from `trace.list`. It excludes stale queued records but does not itself prove the input/record ordering.
`plan='any'` records the observed plan or its absence without asserting a particular value.
The trace and bridge witnesses do not prove that the requested on-screen text was visibly rendered.

`routes:drill_observe(attempts, cap, game='Guard the Edge', perfect=None, allow_stopped=False,
count='terminals')` counts matching release or terminal markers and checks the named game at both ends.
Markers must match the game and have a consistent Perfect bit, optionally as requested; CPU kinds can
change and are reported as a set. It needs an armed records point targeting the
`edge_juggle_attempt` symbol and a trace file on the instance. It rejects trace loss, unreadable records,
point changes and bridge epoch changes. File-loss and point-drop counters are recorded at entry and exit and
must remain unchanged, and every attempt-point hit must have been recorded; nonzero initial counts are allowed.
The first `trace.list` sequence fences out earlier recorded markers even if appended later; excluded markers
are reported compactly, counted markers supply the sequence/frame range, and closing counters must account
for the highest file sequence read.
Zoom arms emit neither release nor terminal markers on this probe and need a separate packet step.
A stopped game fails unless `allow_stopped` is true. The block performs no inputs;
marker counts do not by themselves prove correct gameplay or a visibly rendered notice.
